The equations & teaching notes

Physical prediction for clocks held stationary outside an ideal spherical, non-rotating Sun:

α(r) = dτ/dt = √(1 − rₛ/r)
rₛ = 2GM/c²

t is time at infinity; τ is local proper time. Locally, every clock ticks normally.

Chosen visual mapping for every point on the grid, with Sun position s and r = |x − s|:

x′ = s + α(r)A (x − s)
f(r) = r α(r)A

A = 0 gives the original grid. A = 1 maps the actual solar clock factor into position. Larger A exaggerates its logarithm: ln(f/r) = A ln α. It does not change the Sun's mass or the numeric clock readout. The Sun symbol has an independent display-size multiplier (2.5× by default) applied after f(R☉); it is drawn over the grid for clarity. It is not a scale boundary. The clock calculation always uses the physical solar radius.

The transverse scale is αA, which increases outwards. The radial derivative is:

f′(r) = αA [1 + A rₛ / (2(r − rₛ))] > 0

The mapping therefore preserves radial ordering outside the Sun. Shared vertices and all intermediate edge points use the same function, keeping cells connected without overlaps introduced by the mapping.

This is not a physical compression of space. It is a Euclidean drawing driven by a clock-rate field, not an isometric representation of curved spacetime. General relativity does not uniquely prescribe these cell shapes. In a Schwarzschild constant-time slice, the actual radial distance element is dℓ = dr/α, rather than a shrinking distance.

The Sun's interior is omitted. Grid lines stop at its surface. Moving the Sun instantly recomputes a translated static field. This is not a dynamical solution of Einstein’s equations; propagation delays, gravitational waves and velocity effects are excluded. No orbits, light bending, rotation or other bodies are simulated. Cutaway boundaries are defined in the undeformed grid.

GM = 1.32712 × 10²⁰ m³/s²; R☉ = 695,700 km; c = 299,792,458 m/s. The starting grid extends to ±4.8 R☉ per axis. Its outer boundary also deforms.
